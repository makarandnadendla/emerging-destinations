Off the Golden Route

Almost every foreign visitor in Japan funnels through the same 600 km corridor: Tokyo → Hakone → Fuji → Kyoto → Nara → Osaka → Hiroshima. Do people from wealthier countries go off the beaten path more often to explore other gems in Japan? The data's answer is region dependent: for example, within Europe, more-developed origins venture further off-path; within Latin America more developed nations tend to stick closer to the Golden Route!

Three rival theories, one null — called in advance

The whole design was fixed before unblinding: outcome, eligibility gate, adjustment set and refutation battery pre-registered in SPEC.md. The estimate was looked at once, at the end.

H1 · Acclimation

Development buys comfort: the wealthier the origin, the tighter its travelers hug the Golden Route.

predicts a negative HDI slope
H2 · Status-good

Development buys adventure: going off the beaten path is the status good that wealth reaches for.

predicts a positive HDI slope
H3 · Heterogeneous

Both are real — in different places. The sign of the development effect depends on the origin region.

predicts opposite slopes by region
H4 · Null

Neither: once travelers are compared like-with-like, development predicts nothing about going off-path.

predicts a flat slope

4 / 7 · ACT II — EXPLORE

Mean off-path-ness by origin region — and a trap

No tidy long-haul vs regional split — the levels refuse the cliché. East-Asian travelers post the highest mean first-trip remoteness of any large region (ski Hokkaido, Kyushu), Europeans and North Americans sit mid-pack, Latin America lowest. Tempting to read development straight off these bars — but each bar also bundles proximity, season mix and trip style, and each region draws from a completely different HDI range. Differences mixed together like that have a name: confounding. Before the question can be answered, it has to be dealt with →

5 / 7 · ACT III — IDENTIFY

Before the answer: the causal map

Compare like with like. Origin region sits on both sides of the question: it sets the HDI pool and the way Japan is traveled (proximity, ski weekends, visa regimes). Trip year does the same — the inbound boom and HDI drift moved together. Left alone, those two back-doors would masquerade as a development effect.

So the estimate compares travelers within the same region and year — exactly the adjustment set DoWhy identifies from the full graph. Budget, trip style, length and self-drive are deliberately not adjusted: they are the channels development acts through, and the target is the total effect. Formal version: the 14-node DAG and the identified backdoor estimand live in analysis/assumptions_dag.py — plus a pre-stated gate that only grants causal readings where HDI actually varies within a region.

6 / 7 · ACT III — ESTIMATE

The adjusted answer — HDI slopes within region

Back-doors blocked (region + year) · slopes per +0.10 HDI · origin-clustered SEs · leave-one-origin-out. Verdict: H3 — both rival theories win, each in its own region.

Europe · status-good (H2) wins here

+0.054 per +0.10 HDI (p=.005). Across 27 European origins, higher development pushes first trips further off the corridor. Sign-stable under leave-one-origin-out.

Latin America · acclimation (H1) wins here

−0.104 per +0.10 HDI (p=.002). Across 11 Latin-American origins the sign flips: higher development keeps first trips closer to the beaten path. Also LOO sign-stable. East & Southeast Asia: no credible effect (East Asia rests on a two-point contrast).

7 / 8 · ACT III — POOL IT

Run one regression instead — and the effect vanishes

Same data, same region + year adjustment as the previous slide; the only change is forcing a single HDI slope on everyone. Shown: the full pooled ladder — OLS, then debiased ML (LinearDML) with three nuisance choices, rung 3 pre-registered.

Opposite truths average to nothing. Europe's +0.054 and Latin America's −0.104 enter the pooled slope with opposite signs, so the one-number answer is a confident-looking ≈ 0 — read alone, it would call this study "H4, development doesn't matter" and be wrong twice. And it is not an estimator problem: plain OLS and all three debiased-ML rungs agree to the third decimal. Pooling, not estimation, is what erases the story.

8 / 8 · APPENDIX

Methods & results

Real data. 2,801 cohort travelers · 278k geotagged Flickr photos inside Japan (2012–2019) · 39 origin countries shown (n≥5 suppression floor; everything on this page is aggregated — no user-level rows). Remoteness = inverse OSM POI density (−ln(1+POI), min-max normalized) on an H3 r6 grid; outcome = photo-weighted mean cell remoteness of the first trip (a controlled direct effect at trip #1, immune to repeat-trip selection).

Identification. Backdoor adjustment set = origin region + trip year (DoWhy-identified estimand; mediators left open for the total effect). A pre-stated gate restricts causal estimation to regions where HDI actually varies within region (sd ≥ 0.02, n ≥ 50): Europe, East Asia, Southeast Asia, Latin America. North America and Oceania are descriptive-only — their origin pools have structurally constant HDI.

Headline (per +0.10 HDI, origin-clustered SEs, leave-one-origin-out). Europe +0.054 [+0.018, +0.090], p=.005, sign-stable · Latin America −0.104 [−0.158, −0.050], p=.002, sign-stable · East Asia +0.013 (p=.38; two-point contrast, uninformative) · Southeast Asia −0.002 (p=.81; precise null). Robust to dropping stated-vs-modal home conflicts, to the pooled all-trips outcome, to trip-gap rules 14/30/60 days, and it strengthens under a 2015-vintage OSM yardstick. Verdict: H3 — heterogeneous, not a universal law.

Secondary + refutations. A pooled estimator ladder (OLS → LinearDML with linear / 'auto' / Optuna-tuned nuisances) agrees rung-for-rung at a null ≈+0.01 — under H3, opposite-signed regional effects cancel in any pooled average (DML self-localizes: 82% of its weight sits on the two null regions). Refutation batteries: hour-of-day negative control PASS (p=.305) · E-values — Europe needs an unmeasured confounder with RR ≥ 1.46 on both sides to lose significance, Latin America ≥ 2.18 · stated-vs-modal home agreement 72% (κ=0.69) PASS · DoWhy battery 6/7 (the one fail is sign-preservation on the pooled estimate, which is itself null — there is no sign to preserve).
