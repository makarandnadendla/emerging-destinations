Positivity / common support · adjustment set: region × trip year

HDI Overlap Atlas

Positivity asks: within every stratum we adjust on, does the treatment actually vary? With a continuous exposure (origin-country HDI) and a discrete adjustment set (origin region × trip year), that means three checks: the treatment’s real support inside each region (Panel A — and it is honestly discrete: a handful of origin trajectories, not a continuum), the occupancy of every region × year cell (Panel B), and the residual treatment variation left after the adjustment set absorbs its share — what the estimator actually identifies from (Panel C). Cohort: 2,234 users with year-matched HDI (Taiwan’s 562 are excluded — UNDP publishes no Taiwan HDI).

A · Treatment support, region by region

One row per origin: the line spans that country’s HDI drift 2012–2019; dots are origin-years sized by users. The gate (sd ≥ 0.02 and n ≥ 50) is printed per region.

B · Region × trip-year occupancy

Every adjustment-set cell the year fixed effects rely on. Dashed cells are empty; single-digit cells are positivity’s thin ice.

C · Residualized treatment — the identifying variation

HDI minus its region×year mean: the variation left for estimation after the back-door set is absorbed. A spike at zero means the region contributes (almost) nothing.

Reading: North America (sd 0.004, two origins) and Oceania (sd 0.003) are near‑point‑masses — no estimator can recover a within-region HDI slope there, which is why the identification gate reports them descriptively only. East Asia clears the gate but its support is three origins (CHN low, KOR/HKG high) — a two-point contrast, flagged as such by the leave-one-origin-out check. Southeast Asia has the widest genuine support. In Panel B, East Asia’s 2018–19 cells (and everything outside the top four regions) run thin — the year fixed effects lean on sparse cells there. Data: user_features (warehouse), adjustment set per analysis/dag.html; gate thresholds from analysis/estimate.py.
